Why You Should Never Clean Up Blood Yourself

2026-09-08

After an accident or medical emergency, the urge to clean blood quickly is understandable. For tiny, contained spots with a known, low-risk source, careful hygiene may suffice. For larger spills, unknown sources, or any trauma-related event, professional remediation is the safer path.

Pathogens Survive Longer Than Most People Expect

Hepatitis B virus can remain infectious in dried blood for at least a week under some conditions. Hepatitis C and other bloodborne pathogens also present risk. Treating all blood as potentially infectious—universal precautions—is the standard approach in professional settings for a reason.

Blood Moves Beyond What You See

Blood flows into seams, under baseboards, into carpet padding, and along floor structures. A surface that looks clean can still hold contamination. Professional assessment looks for those paths instead of stopping at visible stains.

Disposal Rules

Material saturated with blood is regulated medical waste in New Jersey. It does not belong in household trash. Providers who perform blood cleanup package and dispose of it through licensed channels. Improper disposal can create liability for the property owner.

Emotional Load

Family members should not have to clean the aftermath of a loved one injury or death. Connecting with a professional crew removes that burden so people can focus on practical and emotional next steps.

When to Call Immediately

  • Any crime or trauma scene involving blood
  • Unattended death or decomposition
  • Large blood spills from accidents
  • Unknown source of blood in a property
  • Any situation involving possible infectious disease
